## 1. Intended eligible users

When authorized and finalized, the contemplated permission covers:
- Individual researchers, enrolled students, educators, and researchers at
  universities, non-profit research institutes, or public research bodies;
- Non-commercial evaluation, verification, reproducibility, coursework,
  benchmarking, and scientific experimentation;
- Paid university staff and funded academic research **only if the work is
  not performed as a commercial development or deliverable for a customer**.

The identity of the institution does not alone make work non-commercial.
For-profit companies, commercial laboratories, customer-funded deliverable
work, product development, paid consulting, production deployment, or
commercial exploitation are **outside** the proposed permission.

## 2. Intended permitted activities

Only for the eligible research purposes above:
- Read, execute, reproduce, and evaluate covered source;
- Make local research modifications and generate research-only
  HLS/RTL/FPGA artifacts where permitted by third-party vendor licenses;
- Share results and publish research findings, accurately identifying the
  software and original authors, limitations, modifications, and relevant
  prior art;
- Share modified source **only within the same authorized research team**,
  with access controlled and these conditions retained.

There is no proposed general permission for reposting/mirroring source,
sub-licensing, publicly distributing modified versions, posting repository
history, shipping bitstreams, commercial services, or distributing private
experimental data. Additional permissions would require separate written
consent from the verified rights holders.

## 3. Attribution and integrity

Scientific publications should cite the actual approved software release
or associated paper, identify meaningful modifications, and not claim
unsupported accuracy, timing, area, energy, security, or all-graph
equivalence. Notices identifying copyright owners, third-party
components, and restrictions must remain intact.

No patent rights, trademark rights, or access to vendor licenses is
proposed to be granted. Hardware outputs may incorporate third-party
rights and must be reviewed separately.
